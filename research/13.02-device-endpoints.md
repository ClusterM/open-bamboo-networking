### 13.2. Device REST endpoints

Endpoints the printer called on `api.bambulab.com` between a cold boot and a finished cloud print, plus the bind-time calls from [§13.3](13.03-bind-flows.md). All `iot-device-service` calls carry the device token and the headers from [§13.1](13.01-device-auth.md#headers). Responses use the usual envelope (`message`, `code`, `error`, [§6.6](06.06-cloud-rest.md#common-json-envelope)) unless shown otherwise.

**Evidence:** printer-side MITM, X2D `01.02.00.00`, 2026-10-22, plus a factory-reset boot, a filament-picker refresh and a second cloud print on 2026-10-25; CRLs re-fetched with the device token on 2026-10-25; 14 hours idle on 2026-10-25/26 ([§13](13-printer-cloud.md#evidence-and-scope)).

Paths below are relative to `/v1/iot-device-service/api/device` unless they start with `/v1/`.

#### Security material

- **`GET …/device/security`** → `{"service_cert":"<PEM>","x509_crl":"<PEM CRL>"}` (no envelope). `service_cert` is `CN=service.bambulab.com`, issued by `CN=application_root.bambulab.com`, serial `1a95f6e871327189da682505c09afc17f50f0992`, valid 2024-07-26 to 2034-07-24. That is the certificate the cloud signs its own MQTT commands with ([§10.4](10.04-mqtt-signing.md#cloud-authored-frames), [§10.2](10.02-secrets.md#cloud-service-certificate-printer-side)). `x509_crl` is an `application_root.bambulab.com` CRL.
- **`GET …/device/security/crls?ca_names=<JSON array>`**, for example `ca_names=["CN=GLOF3813734089.bambulab.com","CN=application_root.bambulab.com"]` → `{"crls":[{"ca":"CN=…","crl":"<PEM CRL>","code":0}, …]}`, one entry per requested CA. At boot the printer asked for both CAs; right after a bind it asked only for `GLOF3813734089.bambulab.com`.

The cloud reissues these CRLs on a schedule rather than per request. In every CRL seen, `crlNumber` read as a Unix timestamp is exactly one hour after `lastUpdate`, and `nextUpdate` is one calendar month plus that same hour after `lastUpdate` (it equals the `crlNumber` time plus one month). All `lastUpdate` values fall at :22 or :52 past the hour, which fits a 30-minute cycle, and requests a quarter of an hour apart get the same CRLs (same `crlNumber`). The `GLOF3813734089.bambulab.com` CRL inside Studio's `app_cert_install` follows the same pattern.

`crlNumber` is the real issue time, and `lastUpdate` is deliberately backdated by one hour, a common allowance for client clock skew. The alternative was that `crlNumber` came from a UTC+1 wall clock (Irish / UK summer time). CRLs fetched after summer time ended (2026-10-25 01:00 UTC) still show exactly one hour, which rules it out. Read this way, the served CRL is always the latest one: its `crlNumber` time was 15 minutes before the request.

The printer fetches both before opening MQTT. After that it fetches both again every hour, idle or printing, always asking for both CAs. Over a 14-hour idle night the fetches came 60 minutes and about one second apart, counted from the device login: after a fresh login at minute 37 of the hour, every later fetch fell at minute 37. Every CRL in that night was freshly issued (`crlNumber` 15 minutes before the fetch), and none of them revoked anything. The earlier fetches seen right after a cancelled print and in the middle of a print were this timer, not the print. The `GLOF3813734089.bambulab.com` CRL is the one Studio also ships inside `app_cert_install` ([§10.2](10.02-secrets.md#provisioning-printer-trust-store)).

#### Device state

- **`PUT …/device/info`** body top-level keys: `ams` (per AMS unit: id, humidity, temperature, drying state, trays), `device_id`, `network` (`dev_access_code`: the 8-char LAN access code), `policy` (`improvement_policy`, `privacy_policy`, `snapshot_policy` booleans), `printer` (`auxiliary_part_fan`, `liveview_camera`, `nozzle_diameter`, `total_print_time`). Response envelope plus `tag_warning: null`. Sent right after login, again once the AMS units are enumerated, at bind time and at the end of a print. This is how the cloud knows the LAN access code it later hands to Studio ([§10.6](10.06-lan-tls-and-access-codes.md)). The code stays the same across unbind and rebind. A factory reset replaces it, and the next report carries the new one ([§6](06-channels.md#access-code)). `printer.total_print_time` survives that reset too: the first report after it was higher than the last report before it. Policy flags are whatever is set at report time; after this reset `improvement_policy` was `true` and `snapshot_policy` was `false`, the opposite of the steady-state report before it.
- **`GET …/device/info`** → envelope plus `dev_name` (the printer name set in the account).
- **`GET …/device/bind`** → `{"message":"bind","user_id":"<uid>","account":null,…}` while bound. After `DELETE …/device/bind` the `message` is `"free"`. The printer calls it once or twice at boot (right after login, and again after the security fetch), once after a bind, and then again from time to time while idle, presumably to notice when the account has been unbound from another client. The idle interval is irregular. One session showed runs about 46 s apart and about 20 s apart. Other idle stretches of 11 to 17 minutes, including a whole cloud print, had no call at all. Every idle call carried the device token and got `bind`. A 14-hour idle night had no `GET …/device/bind` at all after the two at login.

#### Firmware check

**`POST …/device/ota`** body: `device` (`type: "product"`, `version` = firmware version, `module` list), `ams` (one entry per accessory: `address`, `dev_model_name`, `device_id`, `hw_version`, `sw_version`, `type`, `version`), `resources` (`device/ai/model`, `device/xmj2`, `device/filament`, `device/hms`, each with its installed version), `optional_firmware: true`, numeric `sequence_id`. Response: `firmware` (mandatory updates, empty here), `firmware_current` (`version`, `force_update`, `url`, `description`, `status: "release"`, `accessories_versions`), `firmware_optional`, `ams`, `resources` (download packages).

At boot the printer sends several of these in quick succession, each with more modules and accessories as they come up, and repeats the check later.

After a factory reset the report lists `device/ai/model`, `device/filament` and `device/hms` at `00.00.00.00` while the firmware version and `device/xmj2` stay at what was installed. The response's `resources` then carries a package only for the entries the cloud wants fetched. The printer GETs `package.url` with the device headers and no `Authorization` ([§13.1](13.01-device-auth.md#headers)). For `device/filament` that URL was on `public-cdn.bblmw.com` and ended in `.zip.sig`; the body is not a ZIP, it starts with the magic `BIMH`. `device/xmj2` was offered at the version the printer already had, and the printer did not fetch it. `device/hms` and `device/ai/model` had no package in the response; the HMS text comes from `e.bambulab.com` instead ([below](#other-hosts)).

#### Cloud print task

`PATCH …/device/task/{task_id}` events, the model download from S3, and the camera photos and encrypted archives the printer uploads to `or-cloud-device-prod` during a cloud print: [§13.5](13.05-cloud-print.md).

#### Liveview credentials

**`POST …/device/ttcode`** body `{"dummy":"dummy"}` → envelope plus `region` (`"us"`), `type` (`"tutk"`), `ttcode` (20 chars), `passwd` (6 chars), `authkey` (8 chars), and Agora-style fields that are all empty or `null` for TUTK (`channel_name`, `app_id`, `license`, `streams`, `stream_key`, `stream_salt`, `rtm`).

`passwd` and `authkey` match the values the cloud puts into each `liveview.prepare` ([§10.3](10.03-mqtt-field-encryption.md)). So they belong to the device and stay fixed across liveview starts, while the `ttcode` inside `ttcode_enc` differs on every `prepare`. Opening the camera in Studio does not make the printer call this. One print called it as `gcode_state` turned `FINISH`, just before the finish photo. Another called it once mid-print (around 40% progress) and not at the end, and that print uploaded a `timelapse` archive afterwards ([§13.5](13.05-cloud-print.md#7-other-calls-during-a-print)). It was also called once with no print running, a few minutes into an idle session.

#### Owner data for the screen

With the user token: `GET /v1/user-service/user/profile/{uid}` (name, avatar URL, MakerWorld counters), then the avatar from `makerworld.bblmw.com` (`/makerworld/user/<date>/<uid>/<hash>.jpg`). The printer appends `?x-oss-process=image/resize,w_240,limit_0/format,png`, so the CDN's image processing returns a 240-pixel-wide PNG rather than the stored JPEG. Right after a bind also `GET /v1/user-service/my/profile` and the filament-preset list below.

#### User filament presets

The printer calls the user-preset resource from [§8.9](08.09-presets.md), with the user token and the device headers. It does so right after a bind and again whenever the user presses the refresh button in the filament picker on the printer's screen. The refresh sends the same query, with the user token received at bind time. This is not the spool catalogue in [§8.15](08.15-filament.md).

This is the only known way the user's filament presets reach the printer. Studio does not push them over LAN or cloud MQTT, and no offline path is known, so a printer that is not bound to a cloud account has no known way to get them.

```
GET /v1/iot-service/api/slicer/setting?version=01.00.00.01&public=false&private=false&private_base=true
```

The query is the same on every bind, including the one after a factory reset. `version` is the literal `01.00.00.01`. It is not the preset's own `version` field, and it is not the vendor-bundle version the plugin sends (`2.8.0.4`, `2.8.0.9`, …, as `?public=false&version=<bundle>` or the reverse order). The server requires `version` but does not filter by it. `public=false` is the one parameter both sides share. `private=false` and `private_base=true` appear only on the printer's request. Together they mean "only the user's presets that have no `base_id`" ([§8.9.2](08.09-presets.md#892-bambu_network_get_setting_list2)).

The response keeps the list envelope (`message`, `code`, `error`, `print`, `printer`, `filament`, `settings`):

- `print` and `printer` are JSON `null`. That follows from the filter, not from the device token or headers: process presets always have a `base_id`, so none survive. A collection with no matching rows is `null`. The same query with the plugin's token returns the same rows.
- `filament.public` is `[]`. `filament.private` is the metadata list. There is no `setting` object and no per-id follow-up; the plugin's list is metadata-only too ([§8.9.2](08.09-presets.md#892-bambu_network_get_setting_list2)).
- `settings` is `{}`.

Each `filament.private` entry:

| Field | Shape |
| --- | --- |
| `setting_id` | `PFUS…` |
| `version` | the preset's own version string (`1.8.0.13`, `0.0.0.0`, …), never the query's `01.00.00.01` |
| `update_time` | `"YYYY-MM-DD HH:MM:SS"` |
| `name` | string |
| `nickname` | `null` |
| `base_id`, `inherits` | `null` (the filter excludes presets with a `base_id`) |
| `filament_vendor`, `filament_type` | strings |
| `filament_id` | string, or `null` |
| `filament_is_support` | bool |
| `nozzle_temperature` | array of two ints |
| `nozzle_hrc` | int |

#### Other hosts

- `e.bambulab.com/query.php?s=device_hms&lang=…&v=…&d=<serial>`, `query.php?s=device_error&…` and `e.bambulab.com/hms/GetActionImage.php?v=…&d=<serial>` — HMS and error text and image lookups, at boot, also before login. They carry the device `X-BBL-*` headers but no `Authorization`. `v` is the stamp the printer has cached. `v=0` (nothing cached, as after a factory reset) returns `result: 0` with the pack: for `device_hms` that is `data.device_hms.en`, a list of about 2500 English records, plus `ver` for the printer to send next time. A `v` the server already considers current returns `result: 201` and an empty `msg`, a few dozen bytes.
- `makerworld.bblmw.com` — owner avatar ([above](#owner-data-for-the-screen)).
- `public-cdn.bblmw.com` — resource packages named by `POST …/device/ota` ([Firmware check](#firmware-check)). Same device headers, no `Authorization`.
- `public-cdn.bblmw.com/hms/actionimage/<region>/<ecode>/<hash>.png?x-oss-process=image/resize,w_552,limit_0/format,png` — the illustration for an error the printer is showing on its screen, fetched when the error is raised (seen for `05008061`, no build plate, [§13.5](13.05-cloud-print.md#4-task-events)). `<region>` was `us`. The request carries only `Host` and `Accept`, no device headers. The `<hash>` probably comes from the `GetActionImage` pack: the stamp the printer sent with `v` matches the image's `last-modified` date, but that pack was never sent in full while being captured. Errors whose HMS action entry has no image (`0500806E`, foreign object) caused no fetch.
- Amazon S3, `us-west-2`, two buckets ([§13.5](13.05-cloud-print.md)):

| Bucket | Host style | What the printer does | Signature |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `or-cloud-upload-prod` | `or-cloud-upload-prod.s3.us-west-2.amazonaws.com` | downloads the print 3MF (`users/<uid>/models/…`) | SigV4 `GET`, 1 hour |
| `or-cloud-device-prod` | `or-cloud-device-prod.s3.us-west-2.amazonaws.com` | uploads the foreign-object frame (`devices/<serial>/print_fail_snapshot/…`) | SigV4 POST policy, 15 minutes |
| `or-cloud-device-prod` | `s3.us-west-2.amazonaws.com/or-cloud-device-prod/…` | uploads the finish photo (`devices/<serial>/finish_snapshot/…`) and the encrypted archives (`devices/<serial>/print/<code>/…`, `devices/<serial>/event_track/…`) | SigV2 `PUT`, 1 day |

None of these carry the device token; the presigned URL or form is the only credential. The `*.iot.*.amazonaws.com` WebSocket that can show up next to printer traffic belongs to another LAN client, not the printer.
